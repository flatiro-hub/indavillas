1. Who is your guest?

Decide who the villa is for before the floor plan is final, because families, friend groups and retreats need different houses. A villa designed for "everyone" ends up with the wrong beds for one group and the wrong kitchen for another.

What three kinds of guest need from a large villa
GuestWhat they look forWhat it changes in the build
Families, often two or three togetherChildren close to parents, safety, easy mealsBedrooms grouped together, at least one twin room, a shallow or fenced pool area, a kitchen made for daily cooking
Friend groupsFair rooms, one big table, space to stay up lateBedrooms of equal standard, each with its own bathroom, a dining table that seats every guest, sound separation between living and sleeping areas
Retreats and eventsOne large shared space, flexible beds, cateringAn open hall or deck, beds that convert between double and twin, a kitchen with room for a cook's team, parking, and neighbours who will tolerate it

The choice also decides the listing later: the first photo, the title and the amenities guests filter by. A villa built for retreats and photographed for honeymooners will confuse both.

2. What can it really earn?

Check what comparable villas earned last year before fixing the budget, so that any projection you are shown can be tested against what the market really did. The median Bali villa with 5 or more bedrooms earned $46,000 on Airbnb in the 12 months to October 2026 and was occupied 44% of the time, according to AirDNA data.

Use the table to test a projection in three ways.

  • Occupancy. A plan that assumes 75% occupancy is assuming more than the top quarter of the market reached. Its median was 61%.
  • Extra bedrooms. The median 6 bedroom villa earned $46,700, against $43,300 for 5 bedrooms. Larger villas charge more per night but fill fewer nights, so revenue grows slowly with size.
  • The upside. The top quarter is real, and for villas with 8 or more bedrooms it starts at $115,300. Plan on the median and work toward the top.

3. Who runs the house?

Hire and train the house team before the first booking, because the first guests review the service as much as the building. A team that learns the villa on paying guests will collect its early reviews at its weakest moment.

  1. Hire the villa manager first, while the building is being finished. They should know every switch, valve and supplier before a guest asks.
  2. Hire housekeeping, pool and garden staff a month before opening, not the week of it.
  3. Run test stays. Have friends or family stay for two or three nights and ask them to report everything, from water pressure to the wifi in the furthest room.
  4. Write the routines down: a ten minute arrival checklist, a cleaning standard and a night contact plan.
  5. Decide who sells the nights. Running the house and running the listing are different jobs. See who does what.

4. What will the first guests see?

Book the photo day before launch, once the villa is fully styled and before anyone has stayed. The first photos and the first reviews stay on a listing for years, and a villa only launches once.

AirDNA data for the 12 months to October 2026 shows how much the early reviews matter. Of the 2,910 Bali villas with 5 or more bedrooms, half (1,471) have fewer than 5 reviews, and their median revenue is $33,500 a year. Villas with 20 or more reviews have a median of $63,200.

1 in 3Bali villas with 5 or more bedrooms on Airbnb has no reviews at all (940 of 2,910).

  • Choose the cover photo first and plan the shoot around it. See how to choose a first photo.
  • Go live with the full set. A listing that opens with phone photos and a promise of better ones starts behind.
  • Open the calendar from the day the villa is truly finished. Guests who arrive to building noise or a bare garden say so in the review.
  • Treat the first ten stays as a project. Check in with each guest on day one, fix what they mention, and read every review as a team.

A workable launch order

The four decisions fall into a rough order. The timing depends on the build, but the sequence holds.

  1. Before the interiors are ordered: choose the guest and check comparable revenue.
  2. While the building is being finished: hire the villa manager and decide who runs the commercial side.
  3. About a month before opening: hire the rest of the team, write the routines, run test stays.
  4. When the styling is complete: photo day.
  5. When the villa is truly ready: listing live, calendar open.
  6. First ten stays: review each one and fix what guests mention.

Questions owners ask

How many bedrooms should a rental villa in Bali have?

Let the guest and the land decide, not revenue per bedroom. In AirDNA data for the 12 months to October 2026, the median 5 bedroom villa earned $43,300 and the median villa with 8 or more bedrooms earned $53,100, so size alone adds little. Larger villas earn more when they are built and marketed for groups, events or retreats.

What occupancy should I assume for a new villa in Bali?

For a villa with 5 or more bedrooms, AirDNA data for the 12 months to October 2026 puts the market median at 44%, and the top quarter by revenue reached 61%. A cautious plan uses the median for the first year, when the listing has few reviews.
