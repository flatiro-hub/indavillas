The two jobs in every villa

Every rental villa has two separate jobs: running the house and selling its nights. They need different skills, and most problems between owners and managers start when the two are bundled without anyone saying so.

{{img:manager-desk|A villa manager's hands filling in a weekly staff schedule on a teak table, beside villa keys, a coffee cup and a laptop}}
  • Running the house covers arrivals, cleaning, maintenance, the staff and taking guest payments. It happens on site, every day.
  • Selling the nights covers the listing, photography, advertising, pricing and guest communication. It decides the rate guests pay and whether they find the villa at all.

A full management company does both jobs. A revenue management company does the second and leaves the first with the owner's team. IndaVillas works the second way: your team runs the house, and we run the numbers.

What does each model cost?

Full villa management in Bali is priced as a share of rental revenue. Published guides (Own Property Abroad, Global Investments) put it between 10 and 25%, most often around 20%, and some companies add setup or monthly fees. Because it is a percentage, the fee rises with every booking.

In AirDNA data for the 12 months to October 2026, the median Bali villa with 5 or more bedrooms earned $46,000 and the top quarter earned a median of $101,700. The table shows what 15% and 25% cost at those two levels, the same range we use on our pricing page.

Who holds the money?

Under revenue management the owner holds the money. Payouts from Airbnb, Booking.com and direct bookings go to the owner's account, and the owner pays the staff and the bills as before.

IndaVillas never holds an owner's money. That is also why the fee is flat: there is no booking revenue passing through our hands to take a share of. Under full management it is common for the manager to collect guest payments, deduct fees and costs, and pay the balance to the owner each month, so the statement deserves a careful read.

Which model suits which owner?

Revenue management suits an owner who already has a house that runs well and a calendar that does not. Full management suits an owner who has neither the team nor the time.

  • Revenue management fits when you have a trusted villa manager and staff, the house earns good reviews, and the gap is in rate, occupancy or visibility.
  • Full management fits when there is nobody on site, you live abroad and want one company answerable for everything, including repairs.
  • Neither is needed yet when the villa is small and already full. For 1 and 2 bedroom properties a one time plan is usually better value than a yearly contract.

Questions owners ask

Does revenue management replace my villa manager or staff?

No. Your team keeps running arrivals, cleaning and maintenance. Revenue management works on what decides the rate guests pay and whether they find the villa.

Why a flat fee and not a percentage?

Because we do not handle your money, there is nothing to take a cut of. You keep every booking in full, and the fee stays the same when revenue grows.

Is revenue management the same as a channel manager?

No. A channel manager is software that keeps calendars and rates in sync across booking sites. Revenue management is the people who decide what those rates should be, write the listing and answer guests. Our plans include setting up a channel manager.
