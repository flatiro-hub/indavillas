Where 5 star villa reviews come from: scent, light, comfort, clarity

Five star villa reviews come mostly from small things a guest notices in the first hour: how the rooms smell, whether the lights work, how the bed feels and how clear the instructions are. None of them needs a renovation. All of them show up in reviews, and in Bali the villas with the best reviews earn about twice as much as the ones with the worst.

The short version

  1. Check four things in every room: scent, light, comfort and clarity.
  2. Check them the way a guest meets the room: after it has been closed up, at night, lying on the bed.
  3. Airbnb asks guests to rate cleanliness, accuracy, check in, communication, location and value. Small faults cost stars in the first four.
  4. Bali villas rated 4.85 or higher earned a median of $65,900 a year, against $30,500 for villas rated under 4.5.

Why do small things decide a review?

Small things decide a villa review because guests have already seen the big things in the photos. The pool, the view and the living room were the reason they booked, so those are expected. What a guest writes about is what surprised them, in either direction.

A villa can have a beautiful pool and still lose a star to a musty wardrobe. Airbnb asks every guest for an overall rating and for six category ratings: cleanliness, accuracy, check in, communication, location and value. A dead bulb touches accuracy. A damp smell touches cleanliness. A confusing gate touches check in. None of them appears in a listing photo.

The four checks below take under half an hour for a whole villa. Do them as a guest would meet each room, not as the person who cleaned it.

Scent: check each room after it has been closed up

Check how each room smells after it has been shut for a few hours, because that is how a guest finds it. In a tropical climate a closed room collects humidity fast, and the team that airs the villa every morning never smells what an arriving guest smells.

  • Open every wardrobe and drawer. Stale air hides in closed furniture.
  • Smell the bathroom with the door shut and the fan off. Drains are the usual source.
  • Run the air conditioning for ten minutes. A dusty filter announces itself.
  • Smell the towels and the spare bedding. Linen folded before it was fully dry turns sour.

Fix the cause, not the symptom. Air the rooms daily, clean filters on a schedule and dry linen completely. A strong room fragrance on top of damp reads as something being hidden.

Light: every bedside needs a working lamp

Check the lighting at night, in every bedroom, because that is when guests use it. Each bedside should have a lamp that works and a switch that can be reached from the pillow.

  • Switch on every lamp and ceiling light. Replace dead and flickering bulbs the same day.
  • Match the bulbs. One cold white bulb in a row of warm ones makes a room look unfinished.
  • Walk from each bed to its bathroom in the dark. A guest should not have to cross a black room.
  • Walk the path from the living area to each bedroom after sunset. Outdoor steps need light.
  • Close the curtains fully. In a house with five or more bedrooms, at least one room usually faces the sunrise.

Comfort: lie on every bed

Lie on every bed for two minutes, in every bedroom, because guests compare rooms. In a villa with five or more bedrooms a group shares one review, and the guest in the weakest room writes it as often as the guest in the best one.

  • Feel for a sagging mattress and flat pillows. Offer a firm and a soft pillow per guest.
  • Notice where the air conditioning blows. Cold air aimed at the pillow wakes people up.
  • Listen. Pool pumps, fridges and gate motors are loudest at night.
  • Run each shower. Time how long hot water takes to arrive and how long it lasts.
  • Count hooks, hangers and towels against the number of guests the room sleeps.

Clarity: read your arrival message as if you had never been there

Read your arrival message as a stranger would, because a guest has only that message and a tired driver. If one step depends on knowing the area, the guest will get it wrong.

A clear arrival message answers six questions without a follow up:

  1. Where exactly is the villa? A map pin that lands on the gate, and a photo of the gate.
  2. Who will meet us, and what is their name?
  3. How do we get in if we arrive late?
  4. What is the wifi name and password?
  5. How do the air conditioning, hot water and safe work?
  6. Who do we message if something stops working, and how fast will they answer?

Test it once. Send the message to a friend who has never visited and ask them to find the villa using nothing else.

A monthly walk through for the whole villa

Walk the villa once a month with this list, in the order a guest moves through it. It works best when the person walking is not the person who prepares the rooms.

  • Arrive at the gate using only the arrival messagePin, gate photo and entry instructions all work.
  • Open each closed room and wardrobe and breathe inNo damp, drain or stale air conditioning smell.
  • Switch on every light after darkEvery bulb works, colours match, paths are lit.
  • Lie on every bedMattress, pillows, air flow and noise are the same standard in every room.
  • Run every showerHot water arrives quickly and lasts.
  • Connect to the wifi in the furthest bedroomThe signal reaches every room guests sleep in.
  • Read the house guideEvery appliance a guest will touch is explained.
  • Write down three faults and fix them this weekThere are always three.

What a better rating is worth

In AirDNA data for the 12 months to October 2026, Bali villas with 5 or more bedrooms rated 4.85 or higher earned a median of $65,900, against $30,500 for villas rated under 4.5. The drop begins below 4.7: villas rated 4.70 to 4.79 had a median of $61,700, and villas rated 4.50 to 4.69 had $45,500.

This is a pattern, not a promise. A rating alone does not double revenue. But the checks above cost almost nothing, and they protect the part of the rating an owner controls. The full breakdown is in what 1,439 Bali villas show about ratings and revenue.

Questions owners ask

Do I need to renovate to raise my rating?

Usually not. Most lost stars trace back to upkeep and communication: smells, bulbs, bedding, hot water and unclear instructions. Renovation makes sense when reviews name the same structural fault again and again, and after the small things are already right.

Which rating categories can an owner improve fastest?

Cleanliness, check in and communication, because they depend on routines your team can change this week. Accuracy improves the day the listing photos and description match the villa. Location cannot be changed, but describing it honestly, including road noise and distances, stops it from costing stars.

How often should a villa be checked like this?

Before every arrival, briefly, by the team. Once a month, fully, by someone who does not work in the villa every day. Faults build up slowly, and the people closest to them are the last to notice.
