5 questions to ask a Bali villa management company before you sign

Before hiring a villa management company in Bali, ask five questions: who reviews my prices and how often, what will I see in my monthly report, who answers my guests at 2am, what is the total cost with all fees included, and what happens if I want to leave. A good manager welcomes every one of them. Vague answers on pricing, reporting, fees or exit terms are the ones to watch.

The short version

  1. Pricing: a named person, on a set schedule.
  2. Reporting: revenue, occupancy and nightly rate, compared with last year.
  3. Guests: a real rota, and reply times they can show you.
  4. Cost: one number, in writing. At 15 to 25% of bookings, a villa earning $46,000 pays $6,900 to $11,500 a year.
  5. Leaving: you keep your listing, your reviews and your data.

1. Who reviews my prices, and how often?

A good answer names a person and a schedule: "Our revenue manager reviews your rates on a fixed schedule and adjusts them for events, seasons and gaps." Pricing is where most of a villa’s yearly result is decided, so it should belong to someone.

In AirDNA data for the 12 months to October 2026, the top quarter of Bali villas with 5 or more bedrooms charged a median of $700 a night at 61% occupancy. The middle of the market charged $427 at 46%. That gap does not come from a rate set once a year.

  • A good answer: a named person, a set schedule, and an example of a recent change and why they made it.
  • Watch for: "the software handles it". Pricing tools are useful, but someone has to set their limits and check their work.

2. What will I see in my monthly report?

A good monthly report shows three numbers and what they are compared with: revenue, occupancy and average nightly rate, each set against the same month last year. Without the comparison a number is only a number.

  • A good answer: a sample report from another villa with the name removed, sent the same day you ask.
  • Also ask for: what was changed that month and why, bookings by source, and the costs deducted, line by line.
  • Watch for: a payout statement presented as a report. A statement tells you what you were paid. A report tells you how the villa is doing.

3. Who answers my guests at 2am?

A good answer describes a rota, not an intention: who sees messages at night, who can go to the villa, and how fast the first reply usually comes. Ask to see their reply times. The booking sites record them.

  • A good answer: a named night contact, a person on call near the villa, and response figures they can show.
  • Watch for: "guests can always reach us" with no detail on who "us" is after midnight.

What a working night plan looks like is covered in a guest messages at 2am.

4. What is the total cost, all fees included?

A good answer is one number, in writing, that includes every fee. Published guides (Own Property Abroad, Global Investments) put full villa management in Bali between 10 and 25% of rental revenue, most often around 20%, and some contracts add setup fees, monthly fees or marked up costs on top.

On a median 5+ bedroom Bali villa, which earned $46,000 in the 12 months to October 2026 according to AirDNA data, a fee of 15 to 25% is $6,900 to $11,500 a year. On a villa in the top quarter, at $101,700, it is $15,300 to $25,400.

  • Ask what the percentage applies to. Gross booking value and revenue after booking site commission are different bases, and the same percentage costs more on the first.
  • Ask what is extra. Setup, photography, linen, maintenance call outs, purchases made on your behalf.
  • Ask for last year’s total on a villa like yours, as one figure.
  • Watch for: an answer that starts with "it depends" and never arrives at a number.

5. What happens if I want to leave?

A good answer is short: after the notice period you keep your listing, your reviews and your data. The reviews on a listing are years of work, and a villa that has to start again with none will earn less while it rebuilds them.

In AirDNA data for the 12 months to October 2026, Bali villas with 5 or more bedrooms and fewer than 5 reviews had a median revenue of $33,500. Villas with 20 or more reviews had $63,200. Losing a listing’s history is expensive.

  • Ask whose account the listing sits in. If it is in the manager’s account, ask in writing how it is transferred to you.
  • Ask about the notice period and any exit fee.
  • Ask who owns the photos, the guest contact list and the website.
  • Watch for: a contract that is silent on all of this.

Questions owners ask

What does villa management cost in Bali?

Published guides put full villa management in Bali between 10 and 25% of rental revenue, with 20% the figure quoted most often. Some companies add setup or monthly fees. Always ask whether the percentage applies before or after booking site commission.

Is a percentage fee or a flat fee better for the owner?

A percentage fee grows with every booking, so the manager shares in good months and the owner pays more as the villa improves. A flat fee is known in advance and stays the same when revenue grows. The right choice depends on what is included: a percentage usually pays for staff on site, a flat fee usually does not.
